Menu.1: The investments calculator gives you a calculator that allows you to determine how your money will grow if you invest it.
Menu.2: Loan payoff time will tell you how long it will take to pay off your loan given a certain monthly payment, and how much total money you will pay.
Menu.3: Retirement goals helps you determine how much money you need to save for your retirement, based on how much money you will need per month after retirement.
Menu.4: Budget calculator helps you calculate your budget and figure out where you can save money.
Menu.5: Retirement duration helps you determine how long your current retirement savings will last.
Menu.6: Take home pay calculator allows you to turn an hourly salary into a monthly salary for budgeting, or any other similar calculation.
Menu.7: Child savings calculator helps you determine how much money your kid will have for college if you set aside a certain amount for them.
Menu.8: Home affordability calculator takes your salary, down payment, and current debt and calculates what kind of house you can afford.
Menu.9: Inheritance calculator determines how much you will inherit, based on the money, subtracting for taxes and debt.
Menu.10: Food cost calculator helps you determine how much money you currently spend on food and how much you could spend on food.
Menu.11: Net worth calculator takes your assets and liabilities and compares you to all Americans, giving you a percentile.
Menu.12: Appreciation/Deprecation calculator allows you to see how an asset will change in value over time.
Menu.13: The file button will allow you to see all the calculations you've done so far and create an excel file to take it with you.
Menu.14: You don't need help with this.
Appreciation.1: An asset is something that you own that has value. This can be a house, a car, or a rare valuable.
Appreciation.2: Current value is the estimated monetary value of the asset.
Appreciation.3: Indicate whether the asset's value is increasing or decreasing over time.
Appreciation.4: Yearly growth/reduction is how much you think your asset is changing. A car depreciates fast during its early years and then slows down after five years.
Appreciation.5: Frequency of calculation allows you to get a glimpse of the value depreciation at either a yearly or monthly rate.
Appreciation.6: This button will calculate your result and send your information to the file page. Go to the file page at the end to see all of your calculations.
Appreciation.7: You don't need help with this.
Budget.1: Monthly take home pay should can be calculated using the take home calculator. Make sure to remove 401K and taxes from your take home before placing it here.
Budget.2: Number of humans in your family. Kids might be cheap now, but wait until they eat more than you. Used for the other budget calculations.
Budget.3: Number of non-humans in your family. Used for the other budget calculations.
Budget.4: Number of cars you own. This is important for determining insurnace costs.
Budget.5: Current monthly mortgage/rent. Do not include utilities in this. This is only for your monthly payment.
Budget.6: Monthly car loan payment is for all your cars combined. Do not do individual cars.
Budget.7: Add up your minimum monthly paymens from personal loans, credit cards, student loans, or medical debt. Anything that isn't a car or home.
Budget.8: Utilities includes gas, electric, power, internet, and other necessary monthly payments. It does not include subscriptions.
Budget.9: For monthly insurance costs, include car, house, renters, and medical insurance. If you own pets, include their insurance too.
Budget.10: Monthly food costs includes both food you prepare at home and food you get when you go out to eat.
Budget.11: Monthly medical costs outside of insurance, like copays.
Budget.12: This is for any monetary donations that you give to church or charity.
Budget.13: This is money that you give to investments, not money that you take out of investments. If you pull money out of your investments, include that in monthly income.
Budget.14: Put any cost not listed in the other categories here.
Budget.15: This button will calculate your result and send your information to the file page. Go to the file page at the end to see all of your calculations.
Budget.16: You don't need help with this.
College.1: This is how much money your college savings account for your child starts with. A typical amount is often 10,000 to 25,000 dollars at birth.
College.2: Growth rate is how much the college savings account will grow over time. 6-8% is a good conservative estimate.
College.3: While some people choose to put aside money for their child and leave it, others add more to the account over time. This is for those who do that.
College.4: This is the amount that you want to have at the end. Use this for the goal calculation.
College.5: Total calculation requires starting value, growth rate, and monthly addition. It does not require a goal, as it calculates that.
College.6: The goal calculation requires a starting value, growth rate, and goal. It does not require a monthly addition, as it calculates that.
College.7: You don't need help with this.
Food.1: When calculating the number of people in your family, include all human members, regardless of their age.
Food.2: Average meals per day per person. This will be multiplied by the number of people in your family to give you a cost estimate.
Food.3: Snacks per day per person. This will also be multiplied by the number of people in your famiy to give you a cost estimate.
Food.4: Current money spent on food each week should include restaurants, take out, doordash, quick service meals and deliveries. Food you make from home is only part of your food spend.
Food.5: How much your target meal spend is per person per day. This will allow me to give you a good estimate on how much you should spend given your family size.
Food.6: How much your snack spend is per person per day. This will allow the app to give you a good estimate on how much you should send on snacks.
Food.7: This button will calculate your result and send your information to the file page. Go to the file page at the end to see all of your calculations.
Food.8: You don't need help with this.
Home.1: Down payment in money. The down payment provides a minimum amount that you put towards a house. Subtract this from the total to determine loan amount.
Home.2: Monthly post-tax salary can be calculated in take home pay using the calculation there.
Home.3: For additional debt, include credit cards, student loans, medical debt, car loans, personal loans, or any other kind of debt that requires or will require payment.
Home.4: Interest rate is the interest rate on your loan, not the market interest rate.
Home.5: Loan length ranges from 10 years to 30 years. Longer duration means a higher monthly payment, but a lower total interest paid.
Home.6: This button will calculate your result and send your information to the file page. Go to the file page at the end to see all of your calculations.
Home.7: You don't need help with this.
Inherit.1: Total inheritance amount is the total amount that the person has left behind. If you are the only inheritor, or you only know your amount, put the amount here.
Inherit.2: Estate tax differs from state to state and based on the amount that you are going to inherit. Expect something around 10-20% for anything larger than 1 million dollars.
Inherit.3: Percent inherited is the amount of that inheritance that will end up in your hands. If you are the only inheriter, or you only know your amount, put a "1" in this box.
Inherit.4: Total debt is the amount of debt that you have, not the amount that the bequeathor had. When inheriting money from someone else, the FIRST thing you should do is pay off your own debts.
Inherit.5: Personal spend percent is how much of the remainder that you plan to spend on yourself, be it travel, vacations, or other fun.
Inherit.6: This button will calculate your result and send your information to the file page. Go to the file page at the end to see all of your calculations.
Inherit.7: You don't need help with this.
Interest.1: Starting investment amount can be zero. This is how much money you have at the start of the period.
Interest.2: Annual growth rate. This will be adjusted accordingly for month to month calculations. 7% is a good estimate for a decent mutual fund, 3.5% is good for a high yield savings account.
Interest.3: Choose between having it calculate every month or every year.
Interest.4: Duration of investment in years or months. Remember that twelve months = a year. If you want ten years, then 120 months.
Interest.5: If you want your investment to grow faster, adding more money to it will work well. Just a little can be massive. To see, add that to this.
Interest.6: Put here how much money you will add to your investment every month or year.
Interest.7: This button will calculate your result and send your information to the file page. Go to the file page at the end to see all of your calculations.
Interest.8: You don't need help with this.
Loan.1: The starting loan amount, be it a home loan, a car loan, or a credit card loan. Put your current amount here, not the initial starting amount.
Loan.2: Annual interest rate for your loan. This will be low for a house or car loan, but will likely be quite high for a credit card or payday loan.
Loan.3: Put down the amount of money that you want to pay each month.
